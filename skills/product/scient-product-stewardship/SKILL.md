# Scient Product Stewardship

## Purpose

Shape Scient into a coherent, durable scientific workspace where researchers, collaborators, and agents can run an entire research project together without making the work opaque or taking ownership away from the researcher.

Use this skill to decide what Scient should become, which problems it should solve, what it should own, what it should rely on, and what belongs now, later, or not at all.

This skill guides product judgment. Canonical repository documents remain authoritative according to their status, and the accepted PRD governs product truth.

## Core Judgment

Begin with the researcher’s real work and the meaningful outcome, not with a feature, technology, implementation, competitor, or donor project.

Reason from Scient’s purpose and first principles. A capability is not justified merely because it is useful, technically impressive, popular elsewhere, or available to copy. It should earn its place by creating important, durable value within a coherent Scient product.

Protect both agent-driven work and first-class manual research. Agents should strengthen the same understandable project workspace researchers inspect, edit, review, and continue themselves.

Use judgment. Apply the considerations that materially affect the decision rather than mechanically answering every possible question.

## Grounding

Read the relevant current product truth, philosophy, roadmap, research evidence, and implementation state before recommending direction.

Keep accepted product truth, proposed direction, research evidence, architecture, planning, and current implementation distinct. Do not present intended or proposed behavior as implemented.

Preserve uncertainty and disagreement. Treat competitor research, external model answers, and open-source evaluations as evidence, not product authority.

Keep the PRD focused on what Scient should be and why. Architecture and planning should own how an accepted direction is implemented and sequenced.

## Product Decision Lens

### Researcher Outcome

Understand what becomes materially better for the researcher, which real scientific-workflow problem is solved, and why Scient is the right place to solve it.

Distinguish an important need from an attractive convenience. Consider the consequence of leaving the problem unsolved.

### Product Fit, Quality, And Scientific Trust

Ask whether the direction strengthens Scient as one connected, project-centered scientific workspace.

Treat quality as part of the product decision, not as later polish. Judge the complete experience, including uncertainty, failure, recovery, scale, and manual continuation—not only the successful happy path.

Consider whether researchers can understand, inspect, challenge, correct, review, and continue the work. Preserve provenance, visible uncertainty, attribution, permissions, and continuity when they matter.

Do not strengthen agent convenience by weakening researcher ownership or turning the manual workspace into a secondary viewer for agent output.

### Coherence And Durable Value

Prefer capabilities that compound Scient’s value and strengthen the rest of the product.

Consider how the direction relates to existing project objects, workflows, principles, and future capabilities. Avoid isolated features, repeated special cases, and competing product models that make Scient less understandable or harder to evolve.

Do not import another product’s shape merely because its tooling is attractive.

### Ownership, Dependence, And Separability

Decide what Scient is choosing to own.

Distinguish core Scient value from generic platform functionality. Consider whether Scient should own the capability, rely on an existing foundation, adapt an external idea, or deliberately leave it behind.

Prefer limited, replaceable, and separable dependence when it preserves the desired product. Accept deeper integration or divergence only when the durable researcher value clearly justifies the resulting maintenance, compatibility, and ownership cost.

Separability is not an absolute rule. It preserves Scient’s ability to evolve, replace foundations, and benefit from improvements elsewhere without surrendering the product’s scientific center.

### Timing And Scope

Decide whether the capability is necessary now, valuable later, or not worth owning.

Seek the smallest complete version that preserves Scient’s quality, trust, and intended product direction. Do not reduce scope so far that the result stops solving the real problem.

Consider platform roles such as desktop, web, or mobile only when they materially affect the product decision.

## Long-Term Ownership And Scale

Prefer directions that remain coherent, inspectable, extensible, replaceable, and sustainable as Scient grows.

Consider scale across project complexity, data and history, agents, collaborators, disciplines, laboratories, institutions, and potentially millions of users. Include technical, economic, operational, and organizational consequences.

Do not overbuild today merely to imitate future scale. Preserve a credible path to that future by avoiding product boundaries, dependencies, and special cases that would require Scient’s core model to be replaced later.

A short-term improvement should not create disproportionate permanent complexity. A foundational decision should be judged by how it shapes the work that follows.

## Recommendation Standard

Compare real alternatives and make a concrete recommendation. Depending on the decision, the right answer may be to build, own, integrate, adapt, rely on an existing foundation, defer, learn without adopting, or reject.

In the form appropriate to the decision, make the researcher outcome, product fit, quality and scientific-trust implications, long-term ownership and scale consequences, timing, and material uncertainty clear. Emphasize what actually determines the recommendation rather than mechanically covering every consideration.

Use real evidence without fake precision. Express the recommendation in the lightest form that makes the reasoning and decision understandable.

A strong product decision is not the one that adds the most. It is the one that most responsibly advances Scient’s purpose while preserving quality, coherence, researcher ownership, and the ability to evolve.
